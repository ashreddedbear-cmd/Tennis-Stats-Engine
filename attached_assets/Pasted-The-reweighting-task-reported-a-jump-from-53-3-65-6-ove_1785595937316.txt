The reweighting task reported a jump from 53.3% → 65.6% overall win rate and 63.3% → 71.0% KEEP-tier win rate after deriving new DEFAULT_WEIGHTS from a leave-one-out ablation at n=3,031. Two things need to be confirmed before trusting those numbers, and a third before trusting the weights are final:

	1.	Possible holdout leakage. The “held-out” validation set (last 607 rows chronologically) needs to be confirmed as genuinely excluded from the leave-one-out edge computation that produced the new weights — not just split out afterward for reporting. If the 607 rows were part of the 3,031 used to derive the weights, the 71.0%/65.6% validation numbers are inflated by testing on data the weights were partly fit on.
	2.	Confounded comparison. The report itself attributes most of the win-rate jump to richer underlying data (new providers), not to the reweighting — but no test isolates the two. The clean comparison is: old DEFAULT_WEIGHTS scored against the new n=3,031 data, vs. new DEFAULT_WEIGHTS scored against the same n=3,031 data. Only that isolates what the reweighting itself actually contributed.
	3.	Weights are provisional. They were derived and applied to builderScoringService.ts at n=3,031, while the 10,000-row backfill was still running in the background. Edges shifted meaningfully between n=1,500 and n=3,031 (Recent Form and Surface Record flipped from negative to strongly positive) — a similar shift is plausible between 3,031 and 10,000. The current weights are live in production code, not staged behind a flag.
