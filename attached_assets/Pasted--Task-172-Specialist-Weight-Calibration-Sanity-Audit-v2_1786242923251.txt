## What & Why

Three reference cases motivate this work:

**Kostyuk/Swiatek (WTA-Hard):** Raw consensus (4/4 signals) → Swiatek ~63%. Specialist 59.8% Kostyuk (weight 85%), General 84.5% Kostyuk (weight 15%). Blend → 64% Kostyuk. Swiatek won. General, not Specialist, was the extreme voice driving the reversal.

**Rinderknech/Nakashima (ATP-Hard):** Raw consensus (4/4) → Nakashima. Specialist 85.9% Rinderknech (weight 70.2%), General 84.5% Rinderknech (weight 29.8%). Blend → 86% Rinderknech. Both calibrators agree against consensus — reweighting between them cannot fix this.

**Pegula/Shnaider (Hard):** All signals agree on Pegula at 93%. No conflict. But General outputs 99.6% — a tail-extreme probability on what may be a thin isotonic bin — creating stake-sizing risk on a pick that was already going to be made.

**Critical arithmetic observation — RESOLVED, see -1e:** on all three matches, (Specialist% × Specialist weight) + (General% × General weight) reproduces the displayed headline exactly. Confirmed in code: raw consensus signals (Elo, Serve/Return, Recent Form, H2H, Market Consensus) feed a single `ensembleProbability`, and once a Specialist is Active, that number — not the raw signals directly — is the only thing both calibrators consume. This is intentional fallback design, not a bug. Full finding under -1e below.

**-1b. Swap-invariance test** — *NOT STARTED*

**-1c. Isotonic bin inspection (both calibrators, extended to tail outputs)** — *NEXT — CURRENT FOCUS*

*Priority target:* start with the **General calibrator's bin covering the ~37% `ensembleProbability` input** for Kostyuk/Swiatek — that's the bin that maps 37% → 84.5% and is the leading suspect for the reversal. *Decision this feeds:* if the bin is `LOW_SUPPORT`, the fix is a minimum-support floor on calibration bins (a bin below the floor falls back to a wider/coarser bin or to raw consensus). If the bin has healthy support and the 37%→84.5% mapping is genuinely what the historical data shows, the question shifts to what's training the General calibrator — its training population may need restricting (e.g. surface- or tour-specific) rather than the bin edges being the problem. Don't pick between these until the sample count is in hand.

**-1d. No-leakage check** — *NOT STARTED*

**-1e. Final-blend attribution check (gates STEP 0)** — **RESOLVED**

Finding, confirmed in code:
```
generalProbability    = applyCalibration(activeCalibration, ensembleProbability/100)
specialistProbability = applyCalibration(segmentMapping,    ensembleProbability/100)
blendedProbability    = specialistWeight × specialistProbability + (1 − specialistWeight) × generalProbability
```
Both calibrators take the exact same input (`ensembleProbability`, the raw-signal blend) and diverge only in which calibration curve transforms it. `specialistWeight` varies by case (0.85 for Kostyuk/Swiatek, 0.702 for Rinderknech/Nakashima) rather than being fixed. Raw consensus signals only ever influence the outcome by way of `ensembleProbability` — once a Specialist is Active, Specialist+General fully replace the raw blend rather than averaging with it. **This is intentional fallback design, not a meta-weight bug.**

Gate implication (per original spec, now applies): every "disagreement" case in STEP 0 must be narrated as *"does the override disagree with the panel it replaced, and was the override right"* — not *"how does the ensemble resolve internal conflict."* STEP 1's fix options are the override-trust options below, not a raw-blend-weighting problem.

For Kostyuk/Swiatek specifically: `ensembleProbability` ≈ 37% Kostyuk (all raw signals point Swiatek). General calibration maps 37% → 84.5% Kostyuk (the extreme jump). Specialist calibration maps the same 37% → 59.8% Kostyuk (a much milder jump). The Specialist, despite carrying 85% of the blend weight, was moderating the General's more extreme output — confirming the spec's original arithmetic that a lower Specialist ceiling would have pushed the blend to 68.4% Kostyuk, further from consensus, not closer.

### STEP 0 (revised) — Stratified backtest across both calibrators — NOT STARTED, gated on -1a/-1b/-1c/-1d

**0a. Define raw consensus — report both**
- Strict: all 4 of {Elo, Serve/Return, Recent Form, Market Consensus} unanimous
- Loose: ≥3 of 4 agree (Strict will likely be too small; loose gives more power. Report both.)

**0b. Classify every case where raw consensus disagrees with the calibrated blend**

| Type | Specialist vs. raw consensus | General vs. raw consensus |
|---|---|---|
| S | disagrees | agrees |
| G | agrees | disagrees |
| B | disagrees | disagrees |

Within B, sub-tag:
- **B-dominant-Specialist** — Specialist sits clearly farther from 50% than General
- **B-dominant-General** — General sits clearly farther from 50% (Kostyuk case — distance: Specialist 9.8pts, General 34.5pts)
- **B-concordant** — within ~5pts of each other (Rinderknech case — distance: Specialist 35.9pts, General 34.5pts, spread 1.4pts)

**0c. Per type/sub-type, report four numbers separately**
- Win rate — blend's pick win rate
- Model EV — calibrated probability × prediction-time odds − 1 (if only closing odds exist, say so explicitly)
- Realized ROI — actual profit/stake at prediction-time odds
- Calibration quality — Brier score, log loss, reliability by probability band, per stratum
- Also report CLV (prediction-time odds vs. closing no-vig odds) as a market-movement check

Label any stratum with fewer than ~30 cases as directionally suggestive only.

**0d. The check that actually tests the STEP 1 fix**

Simulate ROI/win-rate for each sub-type under the proposed new ceiling (e.g. 65%), separately per sub-type:
- Kostyuk (B-dominant-General): 59.8%×0.65 + 84.5%×0.35 = 68.4% vs. original 63.5% → more confident in the wrong direction. Ceiling backfires here — General is the extreme voice; capping Specialist hands more relative weight to the more-extreme General.
- Rinderknech (B-concordant): 85.9%×0.65 + 84.5%×0.35 = 85.4% vs. original 85.5% → functionally unchanged. Reweighting between two agreeing calibrators can't move the pick.

A single global ceiling on Specialist is empirically shown to (a) actively worsen B-dominant-General cases and (b) do nothing for B-concordant cases. Any fix must address these sub-types separately.

### STEP 1 — Apply the fix informed by STEP 0d — NOT STARTED

Branch on the per-sub-type stratified result, not the pooled result:
- **B-dominant-General:** Fix must address General's influence, not Specialist's ceiling. Options: cap General's weight symmetrically, or add a minimum-sample floor before either calibrator can pull the blend more than X points from raw consensus.
- **B-concordant:** Needs a different mechanism — either blending back toward raw consensus when both calibrators diverge from it together, or resolving why they diverge together (see -1e).
- **B-dominant-Specialist:** Original ceiling reduction logic applies here — but only here.

Since -1e confirmed raw consensus is a designed override (not a blended input): STEP 1's primary lever is *when the override is trusted*, not how weight is distributed once it's trusted.
